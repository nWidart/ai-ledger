Cost answers three questions: what did this period cost, where did the spend go, and why did it change? Every figure is an estimate. It applies API list prices to the tokens in your transcripts; it is not a bill. See [Cost estimates](/ai-ledger/concepts/cost-estimates/) for what the estimate includes and leaves out.

The page follows the filters at its top. Every figure below describes the selected project, time range and source.

## Filters

| Control | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Project | A searchable list of projects, plus "All projects". |
| Time range | 7d, 14d, 30d, 90d, All. The default is 30d. |
| Source | All, Claude Code, Codex. |
| Include automated sessions | On or off. |

The filters live in the page URL, together with the inspected project and the open session. The project filter changes only through these controls, through a project key in the chart's legend, or through a "Filter dashboard to" button in a sheet; selecting a project or session elsewhere opens a sheet and leaves the filter alone. See [Filters](/ai-ledger/everywhere/filters/).

## Section index

The page has a section index (Overview, Per day, Projects, Insights) and two drawers (Tokens, Sessions). A drawer is a table folded behind its title. At 1440×900 the daily chart starts on the first screen.

## Panels

### Overview

<ThemedImage dark={overview} light={overviewLight} alt="Cost's heading with the estimated cost and its change, the filters, and the plan gauge with the month so far, the pace and the plan tick." />

**Shows** the heading, which answers "what did this period cost, and how does that compare?" in two figures:

> $3,429 estimated cost 17.8× the previous 30 days

**Figures**

- The estimated cost of the view. With one project selected, the heading adds "on" and the project. With the All range, it adds "since" and the first day with spend.
- The comparison with the previous period of equal length:
  - from 2× up, a multiple: "17.8× the previous 30 days";
  - below 2×, a percentage: "+35% vs the previous 30 days" or "−12% vs the previous 30 days";
  - "flat vs the previous 30 days" when the two costs are within half a percent;
  - "nothing in the previous 30 days" or "no priced usage in the previous 30 days" when the previous period has no cost to divide by;
  - "no priced usage in this period" when no request of the view has a price.
  - The All range shows no comparison.
- A line gives the dates of both windows: "Oct 22 – Nov 20, 2026 vs Sep 22 – Oct 21, 2026". A "thin history" chip marks a previous window with prompts on fewer than half as many days as this one, since its change then says little. For All: "All time" and the dates.
- With no requests in the view, the heading reads "No agent usage in the last 30 days." (or "yet" for All).

**How measured** ends the line of dates. It is a toggletip, as on every page (see [Data tables](/ai-ledger/everywhere/data-tables/#how-measured)). It holds:

- each window's dates and how many of its days had prompts, and the previous window's cost;
- the sessions (and projects), the tokens and the requests;
- "Cost is estimated at API list prices" (and "leaves out unpriced models" when some are unpriced);
- how the gauge's pace and plan multiple are computed, when the gauge shows.

### Plan gauge

The gauge sits beside the heading, under the filters. It measures the calendar month, not the range, so it reads the same for every range, All included.

**Figures**

- **So far**: the cost this month up to yesterday, as a solid bar.
- **On pace for**: the projection for the month, as a lighter bar that extends it. The projection is the cost so far plus the mean daily cost of the last 7 complete days times the days left, today included.
- **Plan**: a tick at the monthly price of the plans in the view, named in the gauge's title ("This month vs plan ($200/mo, Claude Max 20x)").
- **N× plan**: the pace divided by the plan's monthly price, as a badge. A multiple below 1 shows as such ("0.6× plan").

**Rules**

- The gauge follows the project, source and automated filters.
- Only the agents with a known plan count. When another agent without a plan also spent this month, the gauge says whose use it counts ("Codex use only").
- Without a known plan, or when no agent with a plan spent this month, the gauge shows the cost so far and the pace of the whole view, with no tick and no multiple.
- The gauge is not shown without 7 complete days of history or without a priced request among them.
- It states no savings. Set plan prices in [Configuration](/ai-ledger/reference/configuration/).

### Banners

- **Unpriced models** appears when some model has no price. It lists each model with its tokens. Their tokens are counted; their cost is unknown and left out of totals. To price one, add it to the user prices file, whose full path the banner names, in the shape of the packaged `prices.json`. The next page load uses it, with no rebuild.
- **Prices file ignored** appears when the user prices file cannot be used. It names the file and the reason. Packaged prices apply until it is fixed. See [Configuration](/ai-ledger/reference/configuration/).

A "+?" chip beside a cost means the figure leaves out some requests from unpriced models. A screen reader reads "plus an unknown cost for N requests from unpriced models", and its hover text shows the count.

### Estimated cost per day

<ThemedImage dark={spend} light={spendLight} alt="Cost's Estimated cost per day chart: one bar per day, stacked by project, with the 7-day average line." />

**Shows** the chart of the view, one bar per day. The title reads "Estimated cost per day", with " by model" or " by agent" for those stackings, or "Tokens per day" for the Tokens measure.

**Figures**

- **Cost measure.** Each bar is stacked by the chosen **Stack by** value:
  - **Project** (the default): the projects that have a color of their own (the busiest of all time), by spend in the view, then Other in grey. With one project selected, one series.
  - **Model**: the models that cost the most in the view, up to six series colors, then Other, labeled without the `claude-` prefix.
  - **Agent**: one series per agent (Claude Code, Codex), no Other.
  - A day's total is the same in every stacking.
- **Tokens measure.** Each bar is stacked by token kind: Input, Output, Thinking, Cache read, Cache write 5m, Cache write 1h. In **Volume**, a bar is the day's tokens, and cache reads usually dwarf the rest. In **Share**, every bar fills the plot from 0% to 100%, and each kind's segment is its share of that day's tokens, so you can see whether output grew or shrank. The title reads "Token share per day". Share draws no 7-day average and no peak label, since both describe the day's total; the tooltip, the screen-reader text and the data table give each kind's share and the day's total tokens. A day with no tokens has no bar.
- A 7-day average line, in the Cost measure and in Volume, drawn when the view has 14 or more days. At a day, it is the mean of the 7 complete days ending there; today has none.
- Today's bar is hatched over each segment's color, and the legend adds "Today so far". The tooltip and the label say "so far".
- A label names the peak, in the Cost measure and in Volume.
- The tooltip lists the day's parts, the total, and the unpriced requests. For Other, it lists the three costliest names inside it, with their cost, and "+ N more" when there are more. A project with no name reads "(no project)".

**Controls**

- **Stack by** (Project, Model, Agent) switches the stacking. It shows only on the Cost measure. The choice is kept in the browser.
- **Scale** (Volume, Share) switches the Tokens measure between the day's tokens and each kind's share of them. Volume is the default. It shows only on the Tokens measure, and the choice is kept in the browser.
- **Cost, Tokens** switch the measure. Cost is the default. The choice is kept in the browser.
- On the Cost measure, a project key in the legend filters the page to that project, an agent key filters to that agent, and the same key again (shown with ✕) clears the filter. Model keys, Other and the average line only name a series; they do not filter or toggle.
- On the Tokens measure, a legend key hides or shows a kind. The hidden kinds are kept in the browser. When every kind is hidden, a note offers "Show every kind".
- Select a day, or focus the chart, use the arrow keys (Home and End jump to the ends) and press Enter, to select that day. The Most expensive sessions drawer opens with every session active that day and its cost on that day, costliest first, so the costs add up to the bar.
- **Show data table** opens a named table: one row per day, one column per series of the current stacking, and a total. It is closed on every page load.

### Project spend and change

<ThemedImage dark={projects} light={projectsLight} alt="Cost's Project spend and change table: each project's daily trend, spend with its share, change against the previous 30 days, cost per commit and cache hit." />

**Shows** one row per project used in the view or in the previous period. The count beside the title is the number of all projects. The table is left out of a one-project view.

**Columns**

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| Project | The project. A row opens the project sheet. |
| Trend | The project's daily cost over the view, as a line in its color. |
| Spend | The estimated cost, with a bar and its share of the view's cost. A "+?" chip marks a cost that leaves out unpriced requests. |
| Δ vs prev N d | The change against the previous period, as a bar on either side of a center line, all on one scale, then the change with its sign. A "new" chip marks a project with no usage before. A project used only in the previous period shows $0 spend and its whole previous cost as a decrease. Left out for the All range. |
| Cost / commit | Estimated cost over commits, or "n/a" with no commits. |
| Cache hit | Cache read tokens over all prompt tokens. |

On a phone, the table keeps Project, Spend and the change.

**Rules**

- Every column sorts, by click or keyboard; the sorted header carries `aria-sort`. The default is Spend, highest first. Trend sorts by the cost of the view's last 7 days. A project with no value in a column comes last in either direction. The sort resets when the view changes.
- With 3 or more big projects, the Cost / commit and Cache hit cells are tinted against the median project, with a ▲ or ▼ mark and a key above the table. Cost / commit is tinted from 1.5 times the median; cache hit from 10 points away. The median covers the projects with at least 2% of the view's cost or at least 10 prompts; Cost / commit also needs 3 or more commits. Hover a tinted cell for how far.
- Projects too small to compare (outside that set) fold into one row, "Small projects (N)", after the big projects, whatever the sort. A project whose change is at least 2% of the view's cost never folds, so a large drop stays in view. The row sums their spend, share and change; the change is unknown when one of theirs is. Folding needs at least 3 small projects and at least 1 big one. **Show N small projects** (and **Hide small projects**) toggles them. A project ringed by Compare projects is never hidden. The fold resets with the view.
- When the previous window is thin, a note above the table says to read a change as "more than before".

**Controls**

- **Download CSV** and **Copy** export the table. The export holds, per project: estimated cost, tokens, cache hit, 1h writes, subagent cost share, commits, cost per commit, cost per prompt and the change. It holds every row in the order shown, small projects included even when folded, with costs and shares rounded to 4 decimals. Copy puts tab-separated text on the clipboard. The file is named `ai-ledger-cost-projects-<date>.csv`, dated today. A text cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return is prefixed with an apostrophe; numbers are not.
- From Compare projects, a note says "Sorted by cost per commit, highest first.", gives the outlier's cost per commit and its multiple of the median, and offers "Sort by cost again", which sorts by Spend and moves focus to its header.
- Every sort says what it did to a screen reader: "Sorted by spend, highest first". That includes Compare projects, Sort by cost again, the same sort twice and the reset a new range makes.

### What stands out

<ThemedImage dark={highlights} light={highlightsLight} alt="Cost's What stands out cards: findings, each with one action." />

**Shows** up to two findings, each with one action, beside Models and effort. They are the findings the heading, the gauge and the project table do not already show. The most severe leads, in a wide card; among equal severity, the finding with more money at stake leads. A card is marked "✓ Good", "▲ Watch" or "● Act", or has no mark.

| Finding | Figure | Mark | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per commit (titled "Cost-per-commit outlier" when a project or session is an outlier) | The median cost per commit and the view's cost per commit. | Good when no project is an outlier. Watch for an outlier, Act when the outlier is four times the median or more. | Compare projects. With one project selected: Open the priciest session when there is no outlier, else Open session. |
| Peak day | The costliest day, as a multiple of the median day with spend and as a share of the range. | Watch when 5 or more days have spend and the peak is 3 or more times the median. | Show that day's sessions. |

**Rules**

- An outlier costs more than twice the median per commit and holds at least 2% of the view's spend. With one project selected, the same test runs over its sessions that have 2 or more commits.
- The median covers the projects big enough to compare, as in the project table. With fewer than 3 such projects with commits, the card says "Too few projects with commits to compare against a median."
- With no commits, the Cost per commit card reads "No commits" and its action is "See cost per prompt".
- Compare projects sorts the project table by cost per commit, scrolls to the outlier, rings it and focuses it.

### Models and effort

<ThemedImage dark={models} light={modelsLight} alt="Cost's Models and effort panel: each model and effort level's share of the estimated cost, costliest first." />

**Shows** the share of estimated cost of each model and effort level, costliest first. A row is named `model · effort`, with `· fast` for fast-mode requests, which form their own rows. A share above zero that rounds to 0% reads "\<1%". A model with no price shows "–" and an "unpriced" chip.

**Controls** The top 8 rows show. **Show all N** shows the rest.

## Drawers

Each drawer title carries a count.

### Tokens and cache

The count is the view's tokens.

**Shows** four figures:

- **Cache hit**: the share of input tokens that came from cache, and the share of cache writes that use the 1-hour lifetime.
- **Subagent cost**: the share of estimated cost that came from subagents, with the amount, and the share of tokens.
- **Per prompt**: estimated cost per prompt, and tokens per prompt.
- **Per commit**: estimated cost per commit, and tokens per commit, or "n/a" with no commits.

<ThemedImage dark={tokens} light={tokensLight} alt="The Tokens and cache drawer: the four figures, then each billed kind's share of tokens as a bar to the left of its name and its share of estimated cost as a bar to the right." />

Under them, a two-sided chart compares each billed kind's share of tokens with its share of estimated cost: Input, Output + thinking (thinking is billed as output), Cache write (the 5-minute and 1-hour lifetimes together) and Cache read, biggest cost first. The share of tokens is a bar to the left of the kind's name, the share of estimated cost a bar to the right. Each side has its own 0 to 100% scale, and both shares are also written next to their bars. A share of 0% has an empty bar. When the chart is narrow (a phone, a Project column, large text), each kind's name goes above its two bars. A sentence above the chart names the kind where the two shares differ most, however small the gap, for example that cache reads are most of the tokens and a small part of the cost. When no request is priced, the sentence says so, and the cost shares read "–" and have no bar, not zero. When the priced requests cost $0.00 (models priced at zero rates), the sentence says so, and each kind's cost reads $0.00 with a share of 0.0%. **Show data table** opens the exact figures: each kind's full token count, share of tokens (one decimal), estimated cost (to the cent) and share of cost (one decimal), with the thinking tokens and each cache write lifetime under their kind. A last line gives the requests and how many came from the main thread.

### Most expensive sessions

The count is the number of sessions listed: the most expensive sessions of the view (up to 50), or with a day selected, every session active that day.

**Columns** Session (title or id, with start time and branch under it), Project, Active (pauses over 20 minutes left out), Prompts, Commits, Cost / commit, Est. cost.

**Controls**

- A day chip, "Active on *day*", shows when a day is selected. Its ✕ shows the most expensive sessions of the view again.
- **Download CSV** and **Copy** export the table, as for the project table. The file is named `ai-ledger-cost-sessions-<date>.csv`, dated today, or with the selected day's date when a day is selected.
- A row opens the session sheet.

## Sheets

A sheet opens beside the page and does not change the filters. Its state is in the URL.

- **Project sheet**: Filter dashboard to *project*, Open project page, Open its errors; eight figures (estimated cost, sessions, tokens, cache hit, tokens from subagents, cost per commit, errors per 100 calls, error loops); with a previous period, the "Change vs the previous N days" (both costs, the change with its sign, a "new" mark, and a daily cost sparkline with the split marked); Models and effort; the most expensive sessions. See [Project](/ai-ledger/pages/project/).
- **Session sheet**: the whole session, its figures, timeline, models and tools, and a Copy button for its resume command. See [Activity](/ai-ledger/pages/activity/#session-sheet).
